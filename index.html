Why not just Duolingo

Every app teaches you to say hello.

Almost none of them teach you the one thing that actually gets you into trouble: the local rule nobody explains until you've already broken it. These are three real examples — the kind of thing this course teaches, not the kind we made up to sound clever.

🇮🇹

Italy

Bars are legally allowed to charge two different prices for the same coffee — one standing at the counter, one sitting at a table. The difference is often double.

→ how to ask before you sit
🇯🇵

Japan

Tipping isn't just unnecessary in Japan — trying to leave one can confuse or mildly offend. Good service is already built into the price.

→ what to say instead of a tip
🇹🇷

Türkiye

In an Istanbul taxi, the meter is required to go on the moment you get in — and drivers aren't allowed to pick and choose their passengers.

→ the one sentence that keeps it honest

How it works

36 short lessons, in the order a trip actually happens.

Not "Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced" — a shelf of grammar levels nobody asked for. Three real stages of being somewhere new.

🛬 Stage 1

Arrival

Your first 24 hours — off the plane and settled in. Immigration, transport, check-in, the first hour at four different counters.

☀️ Stage 2

Everyday

Eating, shopping, getting around, and sorting things out — like someone who's done this before, not someone reading a phrasebook.

🌙 Stage 3

Blending In

Telling the story of your day, catching the local humor, being invited somewhere — not a lost tourist, someone who came to stay a while.
